## 2. Executive Summary

Callow Astronaut: The Mars Mission is an educational game developed and completed to convey to children aged 10-16 what it means to be an astronaut on Mars, through an interactive story and a mission simulation. Players take on the role of a young astronaut who has reached Mars, managing base life, exploring the environment, and making critical decisions when faced with unexpected situations.

The decisions made directly affect the status of vital resources such as oxygen, and the player is given three chances to make a mistake. In the project, scientific knowledge is not merely conveyed; rather, the player is given the opportunity to think and make choices using this knowledge.

## 3. The Problem and Solution

### Problem
For students aged 10-16, space science and the challenges of living in space often remain abstract and theoretical, making this information boring. Furthermore, accessible learning environments where they can interactively experience information based on NASA's open data and research are limited.

#### Detailed Problem Analysis (Q&A & Step-by-Step Breakdown)
* **Whose problem?**: This problem might seem to affect only teens, but since science and the future will develop in teens' hands, it actually must concern everybody.
* **What is the problem?**: Datasets and theoretical information are not suitable for younger people pedagogically.
* **Why is it important?**: An interest in space should be instilled in teens by adults to help them to be versatile and curious people.

#### Explanation of the Problem Step-by-Step:
* **Science will shape the next generation's minds**: Arousing teens' curiosity is important for not only them, but also all of us.
* **Teens find abstract and theoretical information about space science boring**: Standard textbooks and static articles explain concepts like Mars gravity (38% of Earth's), radiation, and life support through formulas rather than experience.
* **Without active participation, teens quickly lose interest**: Surviving on Mars requires solving critical daily challenges (such as temperatures around $-225^{\circ}F/-153^{\circ}C$, extracting oxygen from the carbon dioxide-rich atmosphere, and adapting to low gravity). Reading about these hardships does not build real problem-solving intuition.
* **Difficulty in accessing raw data**: NASA provides massive amounts of free, open data (Mars rover imagery, weather telemetry, MOXIE oxygen experiment logs). However, raw tables and scientific portals are difficult for a 10-16-year-old to navigate without a game-like interface.

### Solution
Within the scope of the project, scientific information about life on Mars and astronaut missions has been designed to be presented through gamified tasks. Players gained the opportunity to apply knowledge by taking part in resource management, observation, decision-making, and the evaluation of outcomes.

### Mission

* The three main mission areas (base management, Mars exploration, and emergencies) have been fully developed.
* The importance of core resources such as oxygen, water, energy, food, and habitat safety has been made visible to the player.
* A clear decision-consequence relationship has been built into every mission; critical thinking has been encouraged instead of rote memorization.
* Information verified by scientific sources has been carefully separated from the simplified simulation values used for the flow of the game.
