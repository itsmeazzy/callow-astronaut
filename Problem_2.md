# Problem

## Problem Description
Teens (target age: 10-16) find abstract and theoretical information about space science and hardships of living in space boring. Furthermore, they are not able to access real NASA datasets interactively.

| QUESTION | ANSWER |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Whose problem?** | This problem might seem to affect only teens, but since science and the future will develop in teens' hands, it actually must concern everybody. |
| **What is the problem?** | Datasets and theoretical information are not suitable for younger people pedagogically. |
| **Why is it important?** | An interest in space should be instilled in teens by adults to help them to be versatile and curious people. |

## Explanation of the Problem Step-by-Step

* **Science will shape the next generation’s minds.**
  Arousing teens’ curiosity is important for not only them, but also all of us.

* **Teens find abstract and theoretical information about space science boring.**
  Standard textbooks and static articles explain concepts like Mars gravity (38% of Earth's), radiation, and life support through formulas rather than experience.

* **Without active participation, teens quickly lose interest.**
  Surviving on Mars requires solving critical daily challenges (such as temperatures around -225°F / -153°C, extracting oxygen from the carbon dioxide-rich atmosphere, and adapting to low gravity)
  Reading about these hardships does not build real problem-solving intuition. NASA provides massive amounts of free, open data (Mars rover imagery, weather telemetry, MOXIE oxygen experiment logs). However, raw tables and scientific portals are difficult for a 10–16-year-old to navigate without a game-like interface.
